## Required additions (new Part I; existing v2 material becomes Part II onward, renumbered; cross-references updated)
Part I — A refresher on computability, from the paper's viewpoint (target 10–14 pages):
1. **Turing machines as the paper uses them.** The machine tuple, configurations, one step, halting, time TIME_M(x); multi-input machines and the paper's decider input format (n,x,y,a,b); the description ⟨M⟩ as a bit string and |M|. Worked example: a 2-state machine that checks a = b for one-bit inputs, with its full transition table and a 3-step trace. Then: what "the description of a verifier" is and why Compress can take one as input (fig:compress).
2. **Universality, and why descriptions are data.** The universal machine U(⟨M⟩, x) with the time overhead statement; the s-m-n theorem (parameter-fixing) with a two-line proof idea; the halting problem's undecidability by diagonalization (proof, half a page); why MIP*=RE needs exactly these: the Halting-problem verifier receives ⟨M⟩ as input and manipulates it.
3. **Kleene's second recursion theorem**, stated ("every total computable transformation of programs has a program that behaves like its own transform"), proved via s-m-n, and then the paper's F(F,M,L,…) trick shown to be this theorem unrolled; the remark that the paper's earlier version invoked it explicitly.
4. **Lambda calculus from scratch.** Terms (variables, abstraction, application), free/bound variables, α-renaming, β-reduction with two worked reductions, normal forms, confluence stated (Church–Rosser, no proof), evaluation strategies (normal order vs call-by-value) and why it matters for fixed points. Church encodings: booleans, if-then-else, pairs, numerals, successor, addition, predecessor (with the standard pair trick), and lists — each with a short worked reduction. Recursion without self-reference: derive the Y combinator step by step (the "hole" argument: we want f = F f; build Y F by self-application; show Y F →β F (Y F)); the Z combinator for call-by-value; a worked factorial. Then de Bruijn indices (why our IR uses them) and the idea of *quoting* a term as data (a term is a tree; a tree is a bit string; hence |t|).
5. **Church–Turing as an engineering statement.** The two simulation directions informally (TM in λ; λ-evaluator as a TM), with the overheads stated in words, pointing forward to the formal theorems of Part II. A half-page dictionary "TM notion → λ notion" (program ↔ closed term, input ↔ argument term, time ↔ fuel/reduction steps, description ↔ serialized tree, universal machine ↔ evaluator, s-m-n ↔ Specialize, recursion theorem ↔ Y).
6. **Why any of this is in a paper about entangled provers.** One page: the compression theorem is a statement about *descriptions* of verifiers and their resource bounds; every transformation (introspection, answer reduction, repetition) is a computable map on descriptions; the Halting verifier is a fixed point of such a map; hence the whole proof is, at the outer level, computability theory with polynomial bookkeeping, and this is what a description language with built-in resource accounting is for. State what is NOT computability (the low-degree tests, rigidity, entanglement bounds) so the reader does not over-read the point.
