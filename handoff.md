# Handoff: A Lisp-like symbolic formulation of MIP* compression and its low-degree PCP

## Context and objective

Investigate whether the homoiconicity and metaprogramming facilities of Lisp-like languages, particularly Julia, Mathematica, Clojure, or Scheme, can make the compression construction in the \(\mathrm{MIP}^*=\mathrm{RE}\) proof substantially more transparent.

The user is a theoretical physicist familiar with quantum information, PCPs, and the broad structure of the proof. Do not provide a general introduction to \(\mathrm{MIP}^*=\mathrm{RE}\). The question is structural and constructive:

> Can the verifier transformations, especially the bespoke low-degree PCP used for answer reduction, be represented as a small algebra of executable combinators acting on symbolic programs?

The main source is:

* Ji, Natarajan, Vidick, Wright, and Yuen, [MIP*=RE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.04383), especially Sections 2, 8–12 and, initially, Section 10.

## What has already been established

There are two distinct combinatorial phenomena.

### 1. The fixed-point part

The self-referential verifier used in the Halting Problem reduction is essentially a fixed-point combinator. Abstractly, if \(\mathsf{Compress}\) is available, define

$$
\Psi_{M,L}(d)(n,x,y,a,b)=
\begin{cases}
1,&M\text{ halts within }n\text{ steps},\\
D_{\mathsf{Compress}((S_L,d),L)}(n,x,y,a,b),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Then

$$
D_{M,L}=Y\Psi_{M,L}.
$$

The paper’s explicit \(F(F,M,L,\ldots)\) construction is the code-level analogue of this self-application. The current paper notes that an earlier version instead invoked Kleene’s recursion theorem.

This part becomes very transparent in lambda calculus or Lisp, but it is not the difficult compression theorem.

### 2. The compression skeleton

At the outer level, the paper’s compression machine is simply

$$
\mathsf{Compress}(V,L)=
\mathsf{Repeat}_{L,\tau}
\left(
\mathsf{AnswerReduce}_{L,\mu,\gamma}
\left(
\mathsf{Introspect}_{L,9}(V)
\right)
\right).
$$

Thus it is already a composition combinator. The content lies in the contracts satisfied by its components:

* exponentially shorter verifier runtime;
* perfect-completeness preservation;
* quantitative soundness and entanglement-dimension bounds;
* preservation of the normal-form class;
* closure of conditionally linear question distributions under recursive introspection, PCP composition, and products.

Merely observing that every computable transformation is representable by a lambda term is therefore insufficient.

## A diagnostic toy example

A recursive midpoint protocol can verify a claim

$$
y=f^{2^n}(x)
$$

in \(O(n)\) verifier time. At each level, the prover supplies a midpoint \(z\), and the verifier randomly checks one of

$$
z=f^{2^{n-1}}(x),\qquad
y=f^{2^{n-1}}(z).
$$

This is generated by a fixed-point term of the form

$$
Y\left(
\lambda r f n x y.\,
\mathsf{if}\;n=0\;
\mathsf{then}\;\mathsf{Test}(y=f(x))
\;\mathsf{else}\;
\mathsf{Ask}\bigl(\lambda z.\mathsf{Coin}
(rf(n-1)xz)(rf(n-1)zy)\bigr)
\right).
$$

It has perfect completeness, but for a false claim the optimal acceptance probability is exactly

$$
p_n=1-2^{-n}.
$$

Naive amplification therefore requires \(\Theta(2^n)\) repetitions and destroys compression. This isolates the hard requirement: an iteratable, gap-preserving PCP-like compression transformation.

## Main task

Test whether a Lisp-like symbolic formulation clarifies the answer-reduction/PCP portion of the actual proof.

Begin with the pipeline

$$
D
\longmapsto
\text{bounded trace}
\longmapsto
\text{succinct 3SAT}
\longmapsto
\text{succinct decoupled 5SAT}
\longmapsto
\text{arithmetization}
\longmapsto
\text{low-degree PCP}
\longmapsto
D_{\mathrm{AR}}.
$$

The aim is not simply to render this as a short pipeline expression. Construct a representation in which the essential invariants are visible and, where feasible, mechanically checked.

## Proposed first experiment

Implement or fully specify a small symbolic model of the low-degree PCP in Section 10.4.

For a succinct decoupled 5SAT instance, introduce low-degree extensions

$$
g_1,\ldots,g_5
$$

of the five witness blocks. Let \(F_{\mathrm{arith}}(x,o,w)\) be the arithmetized local-clause predicate and form

$$
c_0(x,o,w)
=
F_{\mathrm{arith}}(x,o,w)
\prod_{i=1}^{5}\bigl(g_i(x_i)-o_i\bigr).
$$

Satisfaction implies that \(c_0\) vanishes on the Boolean cube. The proof contains additional polynomials \(c_j\) satisfying

$$
c_0(z)=\sum_j c_j(z)z_j(1-z_j).
$$

The PCP proof is

$$
\Pi=(g_1,\ldots,g_5,c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_{m'}).
$$

Represent the following symbolically:

* multilinear extensions \(g_i\);
* the arithmetized local constraint \(F_{\mathrm{arith}}\);
* construction of \(c_0\);
* the zero-on-the-cube certificate;
* point queries;
* axis-parallel and diagonal line restrictions;
* the formula test;
* the zero-on-subcube test;
* the low-degree tests;
* consistency with the encoded player answers.

Demonstrate perfect completeness on a small explicit instance. For soundness, separate carefully:

1. soundness assuming the submitted objects are low-degree polynomials;
2. enforcement of that assumption by low-degree tests;
3. the Schwartz–Zippel step;
4. the later quantum consistency/rigidity argument.

Do not claim that numerical testing proves soundness.

## Representation requirements

Do not rely on raw Julia `Expr` objects alone, and do not hide the construction inside macros. Define an explicit symbolic intermediate language. Macros may provide surface syntax, but ordinary pure functions should implement the transformations.

The representation should track at least:

$$
\mathsf{Poly}
[\mathbb F_q,\mathrm{Variables},\mathrm{IndividualDegree},
 \mathrm{Dependencies}],
$$

$$
\mathsf{Sampler}
[\mathrm{DistributionClass},\mathrm{AdaptivityLevels}],
$$

and

$$
\mathsf{Verifier}
[\mathrm{QuestionLength},\mathrm{AnswerLength},
 \mathrm{Runtime},\mathrm{Gap}].
$$

Important invariants include:

* individual and total degree;
* field size;
* variable-block dependence of each \(g_i\);
* number and form of random queries;
* whether a sampler is conditionally linear;
* description size and runtime of generated terms;
* completeness and soundness transformations.

Consider returning a term together with an inspectable derivation or certificate:

```julia
struct Checked{T,C}
    term::T
    certificate::C
end
```

The initial certificate need not be formally verified, but every claimed bound should have an explicit derivation tree rather than an informal comment.

## Broader structural question

Investigate whether the real reusable object is not a particular PCP but an algebra \(\mathcal Q\) of query distributions satisfying closure laws such as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Introspect}(\mathcal Q)&\subseteq\mathcal Q,\\
\mathsf{PCPCompose}(\mathcal Q)&\subseteq\mathcal Q,\\
\mathcal Q\times\mathcal Q&\subseteq\mathcal Q.
\end{aligned}
$$

Low-degree PCPs may be singled out because affine restrictions, random-point identity tests, and products naturally preserve the conditionally linear query language. Determine whether this explains, structurally, why generic off-the-shelf PCPPs do not fit the recursive compression framework.

The paper itself says that ordinary PCPP query distributions are cumbersome to reconcile with conditional linearity, motivating its bespoke low-degree construction.

## Language choice

Prefer Julia for the initial executable prototype unless another language has a decisive advantage. Julia offers:

* symbolic AST manipulation;
* multiple dispatch for different representations;
* efficient finite-field experimentation;
* readable mathematical code;
* a possible later route to generated executable verifiers.

Mathematica may be useful for polynomial identities and rewriting. Clojure or Scheme may give the cleanest account of quotation, specialization, and fixed points. Lean may later verify transformation contracts, but it should not impede the initial discovery phase.

Distinguish carefully between:

* a function or closure;
* quoted syntax representing a program;
* a compiled circuit;
* a universal evaluator;
* code specialization.

The actual compression machine consumes program descriptions, not extensional function values.

## Deliverables

Produce:

1. A concise mathematical description of the proposed symbolic term language.
2. A minimal executable Julia prototype, if feasible.
3. At least one explicit small PCP instance passing the completeness checks.
4. Automatic reporting of degree and variable-dependency information.
5. An explicit representation of the PCP query sampler and an assessment of conditional linearity.
6. A transformation trace showing the intermediate symbolic objects, rather than only the final output.
7. A sober assessment of what became clearer and what remains mathematically difficult.
8. A recommended next step toward representing the complete answer-reduction transformation.

The central hypothesis is to be tested, not assumed:

> Lisp-like homoiconicity may remove much of the universal-machine and source-description bookkeeping and expose answer reduction as a small composition of symbolic transformations. The low-degree and operator-algebraic soundness estimates may nevertheless remain essentially unchanged.

Avoid generic praise and introductory exposition. Focus on the actual construction, executable representations, invariants, and failure modes.
